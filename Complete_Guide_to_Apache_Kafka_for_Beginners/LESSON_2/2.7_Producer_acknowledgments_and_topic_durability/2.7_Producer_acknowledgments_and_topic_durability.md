# 2.7 Producer acknowledgments and topic durability

# 1. Producer acknowledgments (`acks`)

When a producer sends a record to Kafka, it can choose how many broker confirmations it waits for. This setting is controlled by the producer's `acks` configuration.

An acknowledgment confirms that Kafka has accepted the write according to the selected acknowledgment policy.

### `acks=0` — No acknowledgment

- The producer does not wait for a broker acknowledgment.
- The producer sends the record and continues without confirmation that the broker received it.
- This provides the lowest acknowledgment-related latency, but has the greatest risk of undetected data loss.

### `acks=1` — Leader acknowledgment

- The producer waits for the partition's **leader replica** to acknowledge the write.
- The producer does not wait for follower replicas to confirm the write.
- If the leader fails before the record is replicated to followers, the record may be lost.
- This provides more protection than `acks=0`, but data loss is still possible.

### `acks=all` — All in-sync replicas acknowledge

- The producer waits for the partition leader and the required **in-sync replicas (ISRs)** to acknowledge the write.
- This offers the strongest acknowledgment guarantee of the three settings.
- It can provide no data loss in the event of certain broker failures when the topic and producer are configured appropriately.
- It may increase write latency and can affect availability if too few replicas are in sync.

> **Important:** `acks=all` means all replicas currently in the ISR, not necessarily every replica assigned to the partition. The `min.insync.replicas` broker/topic setting helps determine how many in-sync replicas must be available for a write to succeed.

## 2. Kafka topic durability and replication factor

Kafka stores topic partitions on brokers. A partition can have multiple replicas on different brokers so that data remains available if a broker fails.

The **replication factor** is the total number of replicas for each partition, including the leader.

For a replication factor of `N`, the cluster can lose up to `N - 1` brokers that host replicas of a partition and still retain at least one copy of that partition's data—assuming the replicas were successfully created and the failures affect different replica-holding brokers.

### Examples

| Replication factor | Copies of each partition | Broker failures the replicas can tolerate while retaining a copy |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 1 | 0 |
| 2 | 2 | 1 |
| 3 | 3 | 2 |

For example, with a replication factor of `2`, a partition has one leader replica and one follower replica on another broker. If the broker hosting the leader fails, the follower may be able to become the new leader, allowing the partition to remain available.

## 3. How acknowledgments and replication work together

Replication creates additional copies of partition data. Acknowledgments determine when the producer considers a write successful.

- A higher replication factor provides more copies of the data.
- `acks=all` makes the producer wait for the in-sync replicas to acknowledge the write.
- `min.insync.replicas` can require a minimum number of in-sync replicas before Kafka accepts a write with `acks=all`.

These settings should be considered together when designing for durability and availability.

## Key takeaways

1. `acks=0`: The producer does not wait for confirmation; data loss is possible.
2. `acks=1`: The producer waits for the partition leader; a failure before replication can cause data loss.
3. `acks=all`: The producer waits for the in-sync replicas required by Kafka's configuration; this provides the strongest write durability.
4. A replication factor of `N` gives each partition `N` copies and can tolerate the loss of up to `N - 1` replica-holding brokers while retaining a copy.
5. Replication factor, `acks`, and `min.insync.replicas` work together to determine Kafka's durability and availability behavior.

## Quick reference

| Setting | Producer waits for | Relative durability |
|---|---|---|
| `acks=0` | No acknowledgment | Lowest |
| `acks=1` | Partition leader | Intermediate |
| `acks=all` | Required in-sync replicas | Highest |

*This is an introductory overview. Kafka producer acknowledgment behavior, ISR changes, and failure scenarios are explored in more depth in advanced producer configuration.*
